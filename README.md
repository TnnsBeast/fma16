## Introduction
This project implements a **half‑precision floating‑point multiply–accumulate (FMA 16)** that
accepts three 16‑bit IEEE‑754 half‑precision operands `X`, `Y`, and `Z` and computes  

```
W = X × Y + Z
```  

The unit is **fully IEEE‑754 compliant**: it supports normalized and sub‑normal inputs,
all four rounding modes, and generates the required *invalid*, *overflow*, and *inexact*
exception flags. The design was developed for the E154 System‑on‑Chip Design course
(Spring 2025) and targets integration with lightweight RISC‑V cores for real‑time graphics.

---

## Design Overview
The datapath is organized into nine sequential stages:

1. **Pre‑processing** – apply control signals to form “real” operands  
2. **Field split** – decode sign, exponent, and mantissa  
3. **Multiply** – compute the 32‑bit product `P = X × Y`  
4. **Align** – right‑shift `Z` to match the exponent of `P` producing `A`  
5. **Add** – add `P` and `A` to form the 33‑bit sum `S`  
6. **Normalize** – leading‑zero count and left shift `S`  
7. **Special‑case detect** – NaN, Inf, zero, sub‑normal  
8. **Round** – IEEE‑754 rounding (RNE, RTZ, RDN, RUP)  
9. **Flag generation** – parallel computation of exception flags  

Key micro‑architectural optimisations include  

* **Parallel flag + rounding path** – eliminates a serial dependency  
* **Leading‑Zero Counter (LZC)** – area‑/speed‑efficient normalisation inspired by
  the *Wally* core  
* **70‑bit internal accumulator** – guarantees correctness across the full test suite

## Synthesis Results
| Metric                    | Result               |
|---------------------------|----------------------|
| Technology                | TSMC 65 nm GP        |
| Total Cell Area           | 40 448 µm²           |
| Critical Path             | 6.424 ns             |
| Area–Delay Product (ADP)  | 0.259 mm²·ns         |

The adder, multiplier, and normalisation stages dominate both **area** and **delay**.
Additional logic to support sub‑normal inputs and extended internal precision accounts
for the higher ADP relative to minimal‑feature implementations.
